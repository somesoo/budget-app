# Savings

An **account** is *where* money is: a savings account, a deposit, IKE or
IKZE, cash. Its balance is the sum of what's been booked on it. A **goal** is
*what* money is for: a trip, a car, the emergency fund. Part of an account's
balance is assigned to goals; whatever isn't is *unassigned*. A goal can draw
on several accounts, an account can fund several goals.

The app doesn't talk to your bank: it books the change and says which
transfer to make.

## What do you want to do?

Guided steps on the Savings tab for the usual money moves:

- **Save more**: what's still free this month (see
  [Safe to spend](adding.md#safe-to-spend)), a quarter, half or all of it as
  a suggestion, and the goal that needs it most first (behind, then on
  track).
- **I need money**: borrow from savings for something that can't wait. It
  suggests where to take it from: money no goal is waiting for, then goals
  without a deadline, then the furthest deadline, and says what a dated goal
  would then need a month. You pick how many months to pay it back over;
  every new plan puts the instalment aside first, and *From the paycheck*
  pays it back. Open loans are listed on the Savings tab with *Pay back*.
- **Move money** between accounts.
- **Fix goal shares**: set what each goal holds on an account when an
  assignment went wrong. Only the shares change, never the balance.
- **Close an account**: move everything to another account, each goal
  keeping its share, then delete it.

Saving more than the plan, or borrowing, moves *Safe to spend* too: the
plan's savings count as spoken for until paid in, anything beyond them counts
as well, and money taken back out of savings is money to spend.

## Spending from a goal

Every goal is also a category of its own, outside the monthly budget: a
trip's dinner, a car repair from the emergency fund. Spending from it takes
the money off the account it was on:

- out of the goal's shares, the biggest first, so the account's balance and
  the goal's share both go down;
- if the goal has less than was spent, the rest from what's unassigned on
  the accounts the goal uses; what can't be covered is left, and the goal
  shows it as overspent;
- editing the expense redoes it, deleting it puts the money back.

That movement isn't saving less: it stays out of a goal's pace and out of
what a month put aside. It can't be deleted on its own; change or delete the
expense instead.

## Money a goal has that the app doesn't know about

A goal's page has *Set saved amount*: say how much it has and where the
money is, an existing account or a new one (e.g. your everyday account). The
difference is booked as a balance correction and set aside for the goal, not
as a deposit, so it's no part of the month's saving, spending or *Safe to
spend*.

## IKE, IKZE and investments: gains and losses

Log what you pay in as deposits: they're what counts toward the yearly limit
and as saving. The value moves by itself, so now and then (monthly, or each
quarter) update the balance to what the provider shows. The difference is the
gain or loss, shown on the account's page as *Put in / Worth now / Gained*,
and it counts neither as saving nor against the limit.

## Hiding or deleting an account

An account's *Edit* sheet can hide it or delete it. Deleting takes its
history and what it held for goals with it (the goals lose that money), so
the sheet says exactly what goes before it asks you to confirm. To keep the
history, hide it, or move the money elsewhere first. A plan that paid into a
deleted account drops that line when it's next opened, and the amount counts
as other savings.
